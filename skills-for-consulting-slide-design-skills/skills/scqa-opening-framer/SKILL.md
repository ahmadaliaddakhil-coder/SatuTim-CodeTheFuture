# SCQA Opening Framer

## When to use
Use this for the first one to three slides of any deck, or for the framing paragraph of an executive summary. Use it when a recommendation feels like it arrives without setup, or when the audience keeps asking "why are we even discussing this?" Use it when you need a skeptical audience to agree on the problem before you propose a solution.

## What it does
It frames the opening as a short narrative that earns the recommendation. Situation states what everyone already agrees on. Complication introduces the tension. Question names what the audience now needs answered. Answer states your recommendation. The structure makes the audience nod along the way to your conclusion.

## Method
SCQA is the classic minto opening structure. Build it in this order.

1. Write the Situation. State only facts the audience already accepts as true. No claims, no analysis, nothing controversial. This is the common ground. If the audience would dispute it, it is not a situation, it is a finding. Keep it to one or two sentences.

2. Write the Complication. State what changed, what is at risk, or what new fact disturbs the situation. This creates the tension that makes the deck worth hearing. The complication is what turns a stable situation into a problem.

3. Derive the Question. The complication naturally raises a question in the audience's mind. Write that question explicitly. It should be the exact question the rest of the deck answers. Common forms: what should we do, why is this happening, which option wins.

4. State the Answer. Give your recommendation in one sentence. This is the apex of the pyramid. The body of the deck then proves it. Do not bury the answer; senior audiences want it up front.

5. Choose the variant for the room. For a supportive audience, lead with the Answer and use SCQA as backup logic. For a skeptical audience, walk Situation, Complication, Question first so they reach the Question before you give the Answer. The content is identical; the order manages resistance.

6. Pressure-test the join. Read S, C, Q, A in sequence. Each must follow from the last with no logical gap. If the Question does not flow from the Complication, the Complication is wrong.

7. Keep it tight. The whole opening should be readable in under 30 seconds. Cut any sentence that is not load-bearing.

## Inputs
- The recommendation (the Answer).
- What the audience currently believes and accepts as fact.
- What recently changed or what risk has emerged.

## Output format
Return four labelled lines: Situation, Complication, Question, Answer, each one or two sentences. Then return a one-line note on which order to present them in given the audience's stance. Optionally, return the same content shaped as an opening slide title plus three supporting bullets.

## Example
Situation: "The retail unit has grown revenue 8 percent a year for five years."
Complication: "In the last two quarters, growth stalled while marketing spend rose 20 percent."
Question: "How do we restart growth without adding cost?"
Answer: "Shift spend from broad acquisition to retention of the top two segments, which return more per dollar and are losing momentum first."
