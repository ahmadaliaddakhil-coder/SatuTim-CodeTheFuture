# Color and Brand Discipline

## When to use
Use this when a deck uses many colors with no logic, when charts are a rainbow, or when slides drift off the corporate template. Use it to set a palette standard at the start of a deck. Use it before review, to make the whole deck feel like one coherent document.

## What it does
It establishes a small, intentional color system and applies it consistently across every slide and chart, inside the brand template. Color becomes a signal, the accent always means "look here", neutrals carry everything else, rather than decoration.

## Method
Disciplined color on a professional deck is mostly restraint. Most of the deck is neutral; color is spent only on meaning.

1. Anchor to the corporate template. Start from the firm or client brand: the primary brand color, the secondary, and the neutral grays. The deck must live inside this palette. Do not introduce off-brand colors.

2. Define roles, not just colors. Assign each color a job: a neutral for body and most chart elements, the brand primary for structure and emphasis, one accent for the single most important element on a slide. Color by role keeps usage consistent.

3. Make most of the deck neutral. The default for text, bars, and lines is a neutral gray or the brand dark. A largely neutral deck looks confident and lets the accent do its work.

4. Spend the accent sparingly. The accent marks the one thing that matters on each slide: the recommended option, the outlier, the quadrant you are in. If everything is accented, nothing is. Aim for one accented element per exhibit.

5. Encode data with shade, not hue. For a series that needs several levels, use shades of one color (light to dark) rather than many different hues. Use distinct hues only for truly categorical, unordered series, and keep them few.

6. Ensure contrast and legibility. Text must meet contrast against its background. Avoid light gray text on white for anything load-bearing. Avoid color combinations that fail for color-blind readers; rely on shade and labels, not red-green alone.

7. Apply consistently across slides. The same category should be the same color on every slide where it appears. Inconsistent color forces the reader to relearn the legend each page.

8. Audit the deck. Scan all slides. Flag off-palette colors, rainbow charts, and accent overuse. Reduce to the role-based system everywhere.

## Inputs
- The corporate or client brand palette.
- The deck or slide to align.
- Which element on each slide deserves the accent.

## Output format
Return the role-based palette (neutral, primary, accent, and any chart shades), the rule for when to use the accent, the fix list for off-palette or rainbow elements, and the consistency rule for recurring categories. Flag any contrast or color-blindness risk.

## Example
Brand: deep blue primary, warm orange accent, grays.
System: body and most chart bars in gray, structure and titles in brand blue, one orange accent per slide for the key element. A four-level maturity scale uses four shades of blue, not four hues. The recommended option's column is the only orange on its slide.
