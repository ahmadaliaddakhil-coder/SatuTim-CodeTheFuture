# Before/After Redesign

## When to use
Use this when you have a real slide that is correct in content but visually weak: misaligned, over-colored, no clear point, default chart. Use it on a junior team member's draft, an inherited deck, or your own first pass. Use it when the fastest path to quality is fixing what exists rather than starting over.

## What it does
It runs a structured redesign of one slide: it diagnoses what is wrong, decides the single message, and rebuilds the slide with the right exhibit, layout, and color, keeping every piece of the original content. It is the slide-rescue workflow expressed as a repeatable method.

## Method
This skill chains the discipline of the other skills into one pass over a single slide.

1. Read the original cold. Note your first impression: what is the point, how long did it take to find, what is confusing or ugly. Record this before fixing anything.

2. Recover the message. Decide the one thing the slide should say. Write it as an action title. If the original carried several points, plan to split (see density balancing).

3. Inventory the content. List every fact, number, label, and element on the original. The redesign must preserve all of it; nothing real gets lost, only restructured or moved to backup.

4. Choose the right exhibit. Decide whether the current chart or layout matches the message. Swap the exhibit if the relationship calls for a different form (comparison, bridge, trend, framework).

5. Restructure for MECE and reading order. Regroup the content so categories are clean and the eye moves logically. Lay it on a grid with consistent alignment and spacing.

6. Strip and direct. Remove chart junk and decorative color, lower the visual weight, then add the single highlight and one annotation that point at the insight.

7. Apply color and icon discipline. Bring the slide into the brand palette, neutral base plus one accent, and normalize any icons to one family.

8. Produce a before/after note. State what changed and why: message recovered, exhibit swapped, layout aligned, color disciplined, detail moved to backup. This makes the improvement defensible to whoever owns the original.

9. Verify nothing was lost. Check the original content inventory against the redesign. Every real fact is present, just better placed.

## Inputs
- The original slide (its content, chart, and layout).
- The brand palette and template.
- The intended message, if known; otherwise infer and confirm it.

## Output format
Return: the diagnosis (what was wrong), the recovered action title, the new exhibit choice, the new layout, the color and icon fixes, and what moved to backup. Include a short before/after summary and a confirmation that all original content is preserved.

## Example
Before: "Q3 results", a rainbow clustered bar chart of revenue by product and region, full legend, no point.
Diagnosis: no message, wrong exhibit for the comparison, color overload.
After: title "Two products drove all of Q3 growth"; sorted bar chart of growth by product, top two in accent, rest gray; region detail moved to backup; one annotation "Both launched in Q1". All revenue figures preserved.
