# Executive Summary Builder

## When to use
Use this when a deck needs a one-page summary that stands alone, because the partner or client will read only the first slide. Use it when the recommendation is buried at the back. Use it at the end of building, once the storyline is set, so the summary mirrors the pyramid apex.

## What it does
It produces one slide that carries the whole argument in compressed form: the recommendation as the action title, three or four supporting reasons as the body, and a clear ask. The summary is self-sufficient. A reader who sees only this page should be able to make the decision.

## Method
The executive summary is the apex of the pyramid rendered as a slide. Build it last, from the storyline.

1. Lead with the recommendation. The slide title is the governing thought as an action title. Not "Executive summary" as the title; the title is the conclusion itself.

2. Give the three to four reasons. Pull the supporting arguments from the storyline tree. Each reason is one tight line, stated as a claim with its headline number where one exists. These are the same MECE supporting lines that structure the rest of the deck.

3. Add the proof points sparingly. Under each reason, allow at most one short evidence phrase. The summary points to the proof, it does not reproduce it. Detail lives in the body slides.

4. State the ask. End with what you need from the audience: a decision, a budget, an approval, a date. A summary with no ask leaves the meeting without an outcome.

5. Add the so-what frame at the top if needed. One line of SCQA-style framing ("Growth has stalled; here is how we restart it") can precede the recommendation for a cold audience.

6. Respect the eye path. Title at top. Reasons as a vertical or two-column list. Ask at the bottom or in a callout on the right. Keep one dominant visual at most. Do not turn the summary into a busy dashboard.

7. Hold the line on length. One slide. If it does not fit, the reasons are too detailed or there are too many. Compress or cut.

8. Reconcile with the deck. Every reason on the summary must have a body slide that proves it, and every body section should ladder up to one of these reasons. If a body section has no home on the summary, question whether it belongs.

## Inputs
- The storyline tree or pyramid apex and supporting lines.
- The headline numbers behind each supporting reason.
- The decision or approval being requested.

## Output format
Return the slide as: title (the recommendation as an action title), three or four supporting reasons each with an optional one-line proof point, and a closing ask. Note where any reason lacks a supporting body slide.

## Example
Title: "Acquire the regional distributor now to lock in shelf space before the category consolidates."
Reasons: (1) The target controls 30 percent of regional shelf, the scarce asset. (2) Build-from-scratch would take three years and still lose the shelf race. (3) The deal pays back in under four years at conservative synergy assumptions.
Ask: "Approve a non-binding offer at up to 6x EBITDA by month end."
